Design notes

  1. Watcher banners are pinned above the tab content on every Loreum DAO page while an op is live. Queued mints are amber; exit ops are red (M-15 acceptance: the banner appears for admin ops). They come from the indexer-driven watcher (M-17). The independent third-party channel (M-21) raises the same gate events without the indexer, and the banner says so, so nobody reads the indexer as the only alarm. The copy gives the exact window and who can stop it.
  2. The ownership chain answers "who can mint" in one line, using the .auth chain pattern: LORE, then LoreMintGate, then the proposing Chamber, with the guardian as a separate veto-only branch. The Chamber is never the LORE owner. This is the fix for the review's Critical finding, where the Safe-to-Chamber handoff let one person mint about 90M LORE within 2 blocks (M-1, M-3). The owner check is matched against the indexed OwnershipTransferred event (M-14).
  3. Headroom is stated honestly. 89.93M is mintable in principle, but only through the gate at up to 1M a quarter, and moving ownership needs dual control. This matches the capture analysis in M-12.
  4. The cap is charged at queue time and refunded on cancel or expiry. That matches the Halmos property symbolicCancelRefundsReservation in M-6, so the meter shows queued amounts as reserved (striped) and not yet minted.
  5. Parameters are shown as immutable. The quorum floor is labelled a tripwire and not a security control, per the plan's corrected facts: a Chamber that owns its ProxyAdmin can be upgraded to report any quorum. Proposer-only execution explains why each mint takes two proposals (open question in the M-3 ADR).
  6. Exits follow the dual-control option in M-3(i): a Chamber proposal, guardian approval and 30 days. Showing "None pending" as an explicit green state is the reassurance directors and holders need. The collapsed example shows what the red state looks like, without implying it is live.
  7. This page makes zero display-time RPC calls (I-11, I-21, M-15 ESLint ban on wagmi read hooks in mint-gate files). Countdowns use the indexer head clock from I-10 instead of useBlockNumber. Lag and outage states are in app-indexer-states.html.
  8. M-15 must render five op states: queued, ready, expired, cancelled and executed. Today's ops cover three of them, so the legend under the table shows all five badges, including the two with no op today. Expired and cancelled both say "cap refunded", matching note 4. The "#47 reverts NotReady" hint is worked out from the indexed eta, not from a display-time simulation. The drill ops stay visible and tagged: they are the public proof that both cancel paths work (M-13, M-20 step 4). Directors cancel with a quorum proposal; the guardian cancels from its own console.
  9. The template builds a normal Chamber proposal (M-16). The preview is rendered from the calldata using the #260 decoder, so what directors confirm is always decoded calldata. The rationale goes to metadataURI. The path shows the two quorum proposals and the 30-day transaction maximum age (WalletTypes).
  10. The recipient policy from M-7: the vault is disabled in the picker because the gate rejects it on-chain (InvalidAccount). Any other Chamber whose asset is LORE triggers a warning, checked against the indexer (M-7(d)). The warning spells out the sniping risk in plain words.
  11. RPC appears only at transaction time, and the page says so with the block number (#260 simulation gate, reused as is). The diff shows that queueing changes the cap but not the supply.
  12. The authority chain appears wherever a seat acts. lena.eth owns her seat from an EOA, so she has no session key: only contract owners can set keys (Chamber.sol session scope rules).
