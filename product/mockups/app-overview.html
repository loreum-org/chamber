Design notes

  1. Every stat carries its source and block (from indexer · #27,184,300). Today's ChamberDetail makes about 13 eth_calls per mount, plus getTransaction and getCancelled for every proposal just to count the queue badge, plus useBalance. This page reads one indexer query and nothing else (I-12). The lint guardrail (I-11) and the zero-display-RPC e2e budget (I-21) keep it that way.
  2. Vault assets and treasury are separate stats. totalAssets is the Chamber's LORE balance and backs cLORE; USDC and ETH in the wallet don't. Showing them together would invite people to read the treasury as share backing. The review makes the same point about spoke balances, which must never count toward totalAssets (I-20, review "Don't move vault assets to spokes").
  3. Quorum shows the formula and the reachable count, not just "3". Quorum uses reachable directors (BoardLib.quorumFor, PMN-M01): a seat whose NFT can't be resolved, or that the Chamber itself holds, doesn't count. Each proposal needs the higher of its submit-time quorum and the live quorum.
  4. The attention list is computed from indexer rows for the connected wallet's seat and ordered by what this person can do now. It replaces the old "Recent transactions" panel, which listed raw targets and ETH values. Each button names the exact action (Execute #46, Review #48). Interactions between items are spelled out, for example that the pending seat change would raise #46 to 4 confirmations (_requiredConfirmations = max(submit, live)).
  5. The authority chain (seat → owner → session key) opens the page, because it answers "who am I acting as?" before any button. lena.eth owns her NFT from an EOA, so there's no session key: only a contract owner can set one. Copy says "session key", never "operator" (P-8, P-9).
  6. The page never shows a simulation result it didn't just run. Simulation is RPC, so it happens at transaction time, inside the execute flow (#260, txSimulation.ts). The overview only says that it will run.
  7. High-risk items get the red badge and an explicit consequence: an upgrade executes immediately at quorum and has no delay. Risk classification comes from the #260 decoder. The proposal page does the full review.
  8. Gate op #3 and Chamber tx #47 are shown as one item, because they're one decision: the Chamber proposes, LoreMintGate enforces the 7-day wait, and #47 executes after the eta. The countdown uses the indexer head clock (useIndexerHead), not useBlockNumber or the wall clock (I-10, I-26, M-15).
  9. The supply authority summary is the M-15 panel in small: LORE owner, proposer, guardian, cap usage and pending ops, all from the indexer (M-14, I-25). LORE's owner is LoreMintGate, never the Chamber (review "Critical: the LORE handoff…", M-3, D-9 ADR-0003). If the owner ever differs from the expected gate, or an exit op is queued, the badge turns red and a banner appears across every Loreum DAO page (M-15, M-17).
  10. The freshness card explains the top-bar chip in plain words: which block the page shows, the estimated head, and that display RPC is zero. It links to the state sheet. Freshness comes from polling the indexer while the tab is visible, which replaces the Alchemy WebSocket subscription; hidden tabs make no requests (I-10, I-19).
  11. The board summary lists every seat with its full chain, because 5 seats fit. The agent director is labelled, and its seat shows its policy Safe as owner. ERC-8004 identity is display only. "Next in line" shows the exact margin (612,000 − 540,000 + 1), so delegators can see how close the seat line is (I-13).
  12. Activity comes from typed chamberEvent columns (actor, tokenId, nonce), not per-event RPC or getLogs from block 0 (I-6). Each actor is rendered as its authority chain, so you can see whether an owner, a Safe or a session key acted. Submissions show their count because submitTransaction* records the proposer's confirmation (WalletLib calls confirmTransaction). That means a SUBMIT-only key, like #12's, still adds its seat's confirmation. Whether that should stay is a C-17 pre-freeze question. Watcher alerts (M-17, I-33) appear inline. Times and blocks match the queue and proposal pages.
  13. Proposal numbers and Explorer token IDs overlap (#44 is both a cancelled proposal and lena's seat). Copy always says "Proposal #44" or "Explorer #44" where both could appear.
