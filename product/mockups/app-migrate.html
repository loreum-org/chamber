Step 6: what happens to the Safe 6

choose before the ceremony

Keep as a module-less vault

Recommended
  • Keeps its 3-of-5 signers and its 100,000,000 gLORE shares, which carry the board's delegation.
  • No modules, no guard, no other assets. The app alerts on any module, guard or owner change.
  • Redelegating only by a public Safe transaction announced 7 days ahead, written into the Chamber's metadata.

Retire it

  • Only after other holders' delegation replaces the Safe's 100,000 LORE.
  • Redeeming the shares first would remove every seat's weight and leave the board empty.
  • A Safe can't be deleted. Retired means empty, with signers told to reject anything it asks them to sign.

Design notes

  1. This is an import flow (#262), so the header shows the Safe and the Chamber side by side instead of the Loreum DAO header and tabs. The new Chamber is live but should not be managed as usual until the handover. This flow is how the design-partner program brings Safe-using orgs onto mainnet (X-1, M2). The top nav keeps "Migrate from Safe" selected.
  2. Say where Loreum runs today. Per the review, Phase 1 is Ethereum only, and the copy names the product (Loreum), not the contract. Multichain is a gated preview, not a promise. The copy does not say "coming soon".
  3. The parallel run is real data, not a timer. The live Migrate.tsx completes its eight steps on setTimeout and shows random mirrored-transaction counts. Here each row pairs a Safe nonce with a Chamber proposal carrying the same calldata hash. Safe executions come from chamber-indexer (a new source for the imported Safe's execution events). Pending Safe signatures are labeled as coming from the Safe Transaction Service. Safe owners, threshold and modules are read by RPC only at the import and handover preflights. The plan lists "do Migrate.tsx's Safe reads count as transaction-time?" as an open decision. This mock assumes yes, only at those two moments.
  4. No LORE mirrors. The review points out that totalAssets is the Chamber's LORE balance, so LORE sent out of the Chamber comes out of every share. The app refuses to mirror a LORE transfer, and the handover never sends LORE to the Chamber as treasury.
  5. The ceremony is one decoded, simulated Safe batch plus a typed phrase. This reuses the #260 decoder and simulation gate: "Simulated at block 27,184,302 via RPC just now" is transaction-time RPC. The button stays disabled while any check is open, and the reason is shown under it. The parallel-run check stays open until the 14 days actually end (Mar 17 14:00), rather than passing early. Call 2 works because the Chamber has a payable receive(). The live app's "Transfer ProxyAdmin" step is dropped, because Factory.createChamber already makes the Chamber its own ProxyAdmin owner. The "money in flight" check pulls Loreum DAO's #46 from the indexer. A handover that ignored it would strand 120,000 USDC in a Safe that is retiring.
  6. "Module-less vault or retire" is a real choice with a consequence. The Safe's deposit is what gives the new board its weight, so retiring first would empty the board. The redelegation rule mirrors M-23: whoever holds the shares must announce any redelegation publicly.
  7. State the governance change plainly. A Safe has a fixed signer list; a Chamber has a market-driven board. The review's customer research points to founder multisigs moving to rules as the clearest wedge. That only works if the signers understand what they are opting into, so the warning sits next to the mapping.
