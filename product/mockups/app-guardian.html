Design notes

  1. The console detects the guardian role from the connected wallet and reuses the .auth chain (role, then Safe, then you), so the guardian's authority reads the same way a director's does. Saying up front that the signer has no seat reinforces the independence rule from M-2: the guardian must not be the Team Safe, a director or a large holder. The chamber header is role-aware, so "New proposal" is disabled with a visible reason and the cancel export is the only primary path on the page.
  2. The main number is the decision deadline, not a generic countdown. Tx #47 is already at 2/3, so after the eta, op #3 is one confirmation from executing. The guardian needs that fact more than anything else on the page. The countdown uses the indexer head clock (I-10).
  3. The provenance checks come from the indexer: proposal.data and confirmations (M-14, M-16), with no getLogs for display. A pending Chamber upgrade (#48) is flagged even though it is unrelated, because a Chamber that owns its ProxyAdmin can be upgraded to change what the proposer does (review Critical finding; corrected facts in the mint-gate plan).
  4. Cancel is destructive: a ghost .btn.danger plus a typed phrase (Design System v1.0, epic #258). The result is an export for the 3-of-5 to sign, not a transaction sent from the app (M-16: the Safe JSON imports into the Transaction Builder).
  5. RPC is used only at export time, for the simulation and the Safe nonce, and the page names the block. Everything else on the page is indexer data (pinned rule; I-11, I-21).
  6. Each signer compares the safeTxHash, domain hash and message hash on their own device. This is the same practice M-10/M-11 require for the handoff. The hashes are real EIP-712 values for this illustrative Safe, gate and nonce, computed with cast.
  7. Exits need guardian approval under the dual-control option in M-3(i). The empty state explains that not approving is itself a veto, which is the liveness-for-safety trade the ADR accepts.
  8. The feed mirrors the watcher (M-17): gate ops, supply and owner changes, session keys, indexer staleness and the weekly heartbeat. The independent second channel (M-21) is shown as a peer, so the indexer is never the only alarm. "Live" means the op behind the alert can still execute. The 72-hour review target from M-2(c) is tracked where signers acknowledge alerts, in the alert channel, because acknowledgements are off-chain state the indexer does not hold. The page therefore does not claim a review status it cannot know.
  9. Every "cannot" line is backed by a test: symbolicGuardianCannotMint and the related Halmos properties (M-6), and the invariants in M-5. The console does not make claims the contract cannot keep.
  10. The owner check pushes the guardian to verify against a second source before signing, and says what to do when the sources disagree. The guardian's job exists for the case where something upstream is wrong. The Team Safe card tracks the SEC-17 threshold hardening (M-2) as a capture signal.
  11. The runbook links point to the guardian playbook sections in the rewritten M1 runbook (M-11). They are not mocked here.
