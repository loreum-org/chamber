One step, and it cannot be undone

LORE uses single-step Ownable. transferOwnership takes effect in the same transaction: there is no accept step and no undo. If the new owner address is wrong, LORE's mint authority is lost or handed to someone else for good. After it executes, only LoreMintGate can mint: every mint waits 7 days in public, the guardian can cancel it, and at most 1,000,000 LORE can be minted a quarter. Moving ownership again takes a Chamber proposal, guardian approval and 30 days.

Design notes

  1. Read-only by construction: there is no connect-wallet prompt and no sign or send button, and the wallet chip says so. This mirrors the M-10 script, which refuses --broadcast. The signature happens in Safe{Wallet}, as M-20 step 6 requires (Chad-only).
  2. The overall status is one sentence with a tally and the earliest time it can turn green, taken from the waiting check. The result is only valid for a short time and must be run again at the latest block on signing day (M-20 step 5; M-11 step 9 archives the output).
  3. The checks follow the M-10 scope: seats from getTop(getSeats()), the EIP-1967 implementation slot, ProxyAdmin.owner(), !paused(), and a scan of pending Chamber transactions for the ProxyAdmin, the Chamber, the gate or LORE. Reachability is backed by on-chain evidence (drill confirmations), not by a claim.
  4. This is a post-audit page, so the audit line appears, but only as a clearly marked placeholder (product rule 3; M-19). Bytecode is matched against a clean build of the audited commit.
  5. Maturity is 7 days of wall-clock seat tenure. SEATING_DELAY is only 1 block, which is the gap the review's Critical finding exploits. The side card warns that a seat change resets the clock, using the next-in-line data.
  6. The capture-cost check is the M-10 weight margin, taken from the review's ECO-3 finding and the M-12 analysis. An outsider needs 3 Explorers that each outweigh the rank-3 seat, and that total is compared with all LORE outside the Team Safe. Seat weights are cLORE shares, so they are converted with convertToAssets before comparing, which fixes the roughly 1,000× unit error the plan review found. The faint line says what the check does not cover: the Team Safe's own power to redelegate is governed by the M-23 delegation plan, not by this script.
  7. Post-handoff steps come from M-20 steps 7 and 8, including the proof that the Team Safe can no longer mint.
  8. This is the one page where display data uses RPC, because it is the preflight right before a signature, which the pinned rule allows. The page says so, and the reads run only when you press Run. The board table comes from the indexer and is labelled that way. The preflight reads the same seats again over RPC, and any mismatch between the two sources fails the run, because it means one of them is wrong (I-10).
  9. Weights are shown in LORE with cLORE shares underneath: M-10 requires convertToAssets and both units (_decimalsOffset = 3), and the capture-cost check (note 6) uses the converted values. The session-key scope bits use the Chamber.sol values, and none of them grants CONFIRM or EXECUTE.
  10. This is the exact transaction M-10 prints. The calldata, domain hash, message hash and safeTxHash are real EIP-712 values for the real Team Safe and LORE addresses, the illustrative gate address and an illustrative nonce, computed with cast. The red banner states the single-step, irreversible nature in plain words, because this is the step the review found unsafe when it pointed at the Chamber (M-1).
  11. The signer checklist puts the M-11 requirement (every signer compares the hash on their hardware device) into steps anyone can follow, and tells signers to compare full addresses rather than the first and last few characters.
