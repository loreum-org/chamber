What the Loreum team can and can't do 3

The team acts through one Safe, 4-of-7, with hardware keys. Each line says what enforces it, so you can check it yourself.

The team can't

  • Can'tMint LORELORE's owner is LoreMintGate. Only the Loreum DAO Chamber can queue a mint, by board quorum, and each waits 7 days.
  • Can'tMove funds out of any ChamberOnly a quorum of that Chamber's seated directors can execute its transactions. Being a Team Safe signer gives no seat.
  • Can'tUpgrade or pause an existing ChamberEach Chamber owns its own ProxyAdmin, so upgrades are that Chamber's own quorum transactions, like Loreum DAO #48.
  • Can'tTake LORE back from the gateMoving LORE's ownership off the gate, renouncing it, or changing the gate's proposer or guardian needs a Chamber proposal, guardian approval and a 30-day wait.
  • Can'tSeat or remove directorsSeats follow delegation. The team can only delegate its own LORE, like any holder.

The team can, in public

  • CanChange what new Chambers are created fromThe Factory's owner is a TimelockController with a 48-hour delay. The Team Safe proposes and executes, so every change is visible for 48 hours first. Existing Chambers aren't affected.
  • CanChange the Explorers mint priceThe Team Safe owns the Explorers NFT contract. The mint page re-checks the price right before you sign.
  • CanInfluence who is seated in Loreum DAOThe Team Safe is a large LORE holder, and its delegation counts like anyone's. That's why no guardian signer is a team signer.
  • CanRun the app, indexer and watcherIf they stop, contracts keep working. This page and the app show the last known state and say so.

Known limits 9

Trade-offs we chose or haven't fixed yet. They're also in the audit's known-issues list.

  • No delay on Chamber transactionsOnce a quorum confirms, any director can execute right away. Watch the queue. LORE mints have their own 7-day delay in the gate.
  • Seats can change after 1 blockA large new delegation can change the board quickly. Changing the number of seats waits 7 days.
  • The guardian can stall mintsIt can cancel every mint. We accept that risk to liveness rather than letting anyone mint faster.
  • Team LORE affects the boardThe Team Safe holds a large share of LORE, so its delegation affects who is seated in Loreum DAO.
  • We run the indexerIf it's down or behind, the app and this page show the last known state and say so. Check Etherscan for anything urgent.
  • Older contracts are outside the auditLORE and the Explorers NFT were deployed before the audit and are outside its scope.

Design notes

  1. Indexer-only page. Every live value is read from chamber-indexer through the chain-aware client with a head clock (I-10), and the page makes zero RPC calls (I-21). Static content (addresses from mainnet.txt, audit and bounty text) is published with the page, and each section's source chip says which is which. The overall card shows head, indexed block and lag. When the indexer lags or stops, the page falls back to the lag and down states defined in _mock-data.md, never to RPC.
  2. Status components and history. The 90-day bar comes from the I-3 staleness monitor. The Mar 14 lag incident matches the watcher feed. The Watcher row shows the M-17 weekly heartbeat, so a disabled workflow gets noticed. Base and Arbitrum are shown as not indexed because Phase 2 is gated. The p95 latency is a mock value.
  3. The can/can't lists turn the review's trust model into checkable claims. C-6: the Factory is owned by a TimelockController (48h) with the Team Safe as proposer and executor, since the Factory owner can retarget every future create. M-2: 4-of-7 Safe and an independent 3-of-5 guardian. Each Chamber owns its own ProxyAdmin. The "can" column says plainly that the team's LORE holdings influence seating. Each line is marked with a word ("Can't", "Can") instead of a check mark, which read as "yes" next to "Mint LORE". The exit line names what the gate spec actually gates: moving LORE's ownership, renouncing it, and changing the proposer or guardian.
  4. Supply authority (M-14/M-15): LORE is owned by LoreMintGate, never directly by the Chamber. The spec's parameters are shown, including dual-control exits (M-3), so the guardian is labelled "can't mint" rather than "cancel only": it also has to approve exits. The ops table matches app-supply-authority.html row for row, including the two 1-LORE cancel drills (#0 guardian cancel, #1 Chamber cancel). Publishing the drills shows both cancel paths were exercised before real mints. Op #3 links to Loreum DAO tx #47, which can't run before the eta. Directors and the guardian are sent to the app screens that act on these ops. The Feb 19 ownership change is in the alert feed, as on app-guardian.html.
  5. Contracts table. Real addresses (LORE, Explorers, Team Safe) are shown in full, selectable and copyable. Illustrative ones are tagged, per _mock-data.md. The implementation and library addresses aren't in the mock data, so the page points to deployments/mainnet.txt instead of inventing them. There are two libraries (BoardLib, WalletLib) because C-1 moves the session-key setter into BoardLib rather than a new SessionLib. Verification badges are mock statuses; they aren't indexer data, so the source chip only claims the owners come from the indexer. The Registry is marked not-for-mainnet (C-9).
  6. Audit wording is the placeholder line only (C-13; D-6/D-7 keep "audited" out of public copy). The scope and out-of-scope lists follow C-12. The page says what the AI-agent reviews were and weren't. The bounty follows C-14/D-19: rewards are in USD or ETH because LORE has no market.
  7. Disclosure channel from SECURITY.md (D-16). The email is an obvious placeholder that Chad must confirm before launch; the mockup doesn't invent a real contact. It is selectable text with a copy button. Response targets are placeholders too.
  8. Alerts use two channels. The indexer-driven watcher (M-17, I-33's lore-mint issues) and the independent Etherscan/Tenderly rules (M-21) watch the same events. Feed entries match the watcher feed in _mock-data.md, including the "no change" checks. The Explorer #31 key entry names the real setter: only the NFT's contract owner can call setDirectorOperator, so the policy Safe executed it after the board co-signed in #45, as app-queue.html records. The filter chips are client-side only.
  9. Known limits come from the audit brief's known-issues register (C-11, D-13): SEATING_DELAY = 1 block, no execution timelock, guardian liveness risk (M-3) and Team Safe LORE concentration (M-2, M-12). Publishing them is what makes the rest of the page believable.
