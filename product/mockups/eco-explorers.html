What an Explorer does 4

An Explorer can hold a board seat in a Chamber when LORE holders delegate to it.

Owning one makes you eligible. On its own it doesn't give you a seat, a vote or income. Delegation decides who sits, and delegators can move their weight whenever they like.

  1. #63 · nadia.eth · not seated

    Hold an Explorer

    Mint one here or buy one from a holder. Chambers that use Loreum Explorers as their membership NFT, like Loreum DAO, can seat it.

  2. 540,000 LORE delegated to #63

    LORE holders delegate

    Holders deposit LORE into a Chamber's vault and delegate that weight to the Explorers they trust. They can move it at any time.

    The top 5 take the seats

    In Loreum DAO the 5 Explorers with the most delegated LORE are the board, and any 3 of them can approve a transaction. Seat changes take effect after 1 block.

Who acts for a seat 5

The owner of a seated Explorer acts for it. An owner that is a contract, like a Safe, can also add a session key that is limited to certain actions and expires. Treasury Agent, an AI director, submits with its key and confirms only through its policy account.

Design notes

  1. Full-bleed art hero. Chad's direction for Explorers is a bold, art-forward showcase in the Art Blocks / Azuki register, not the operator console. The newest mint (#1247) fills the viewport, with a slow drift, pointer parallax and twinkling stars. Everything stops under prefers-reduced-motion. The art on this page is generated in the browser as a placeholder for the real IPFS images, and the Form/Palette labels are placeholder traits, not the collection's metadata.
  2. Mint panel. Name, supply, price and the per-wallet limit are read from LoreumNFT (totalSupply, MAX_SUPPLY, mintCost, MAX_MINT), as Claim.tsx does. The Explorers site reads the NFT contract and IPFS directly because it has no collection indexer; the app-only indexer rule doesn't apply here. It reads once on page load and never polls or watches, so an idle tab costs no RPC; the source line under the panel says so. The mint is simulated at click time ("Simulated at block … via RPC just now"), and a price change is shown as a designed state, not a toast. The minted count (1,247) is a mock value and isn't in _mock-data.md; on-chain supply was 70 in Sep 2026 (review read cited in I-6).
  3. Recent-mints marquee and grid. Token IDs are sequential, so "newest first" is totalSupply counting down. This matches Home.tsx (FEATURED_COUNT = 8) and costs no enumeration. The marquee pauses on hover and focus, and its duplicate track is hidden from assistive tech.
  4. Honest statement of what an Explorer does (D-6). The page never says the NFT is a seat and makes no yield or income claims. It uses the exact framing: an Explorer can hold a seat when LORE holders delegate to it. The ranked bars show the real rule (top 5 by delegated LORE, quorum 3 of 5 from 1 + n*51/100, SEATING_DELAY = 1 block).
  5. The authority chain carries over from the app's signature element (seat, then owner, then session key) in showcase styling. The copy says "session key", never "operator" (P-8/P-9). Treasury Agent's scope 0x35 omits CONFIRM because it confirms only through its policy account.
  6. Board wall from chamber-indexer. Seats, owners and delegated LORE come from chamber-indexer, the same GraphQL API the app uses (I-6 NFT ownership, I-13 board and delegation), so showing the board costs no RPC. The board wall and the seat status on My Explorers cards are the site's only indexer reads, and the freshness chip says where the data came from. All values match _mock-data.md.
  7. The full public gallery is out of scope. Browsing and filtering all 10,000 tokens by trait needs a collection index. The card says so plainly and points to what does exist (My Explorers) instead of a disabled "later" button or a link to a gallery that doesn't exist.
  8. My Explorers stays wallet-scoped (tokenOfOwnerByIndex, as in Gallery.tsx). Seat status on each card comes from the indexer. lena.eth owns #44 from an EOA, so the card explains why there's no session key rather than offering a button that would fail (setDirectorOperator reverts unless the owner has code). The owner and key nodes carry their detail on a second line so nothing truncates in the compact chain.
  9. Contract facts come from explorers/deployments/mainnet.txt: owner Team Safe, MAX_MINT 100, royalty 500 bps. Because the owner can change the price, the page says so next to the price and explains the pre-sign check. The Team Safe threshold is 4-of-7 after M-2.
  10. Trust callout replaces the Story page's audit claim (Story.tsx:161, D-6; the D-7 banned-claims check keeps it from returning). It links to eco-trust.html instead of asserting anything.
