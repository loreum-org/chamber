How it works 3

Delegation picks the board. The board runs one queue.

A Chamber is the contract Loreum deploys for your organization: a vault, a ranked board and a quorum wallet in one. The rules below are enforced by that contract, not by us.

  1. 1

    Delegate

    Holders deposit the Chamber's token into its vault and delegate that weight to membership NFTs. They can undelegate at any time.

    nadia.eth's Explorer
    #63 · 540,000 LORE
  2. 2

    Seat

    The NFTs with the most delegated weight hold the seats. Whoever owns a seated NFT is a director, whether a person, a Safe or an AI agent's policy account.

    Seat 5 line
    612,000 LORE (#58)
  3. 3

    Queue

    Any director submits a transaction. Everyone sees the decoded call and a simulation of what it changes before they confirm.

    treasury −120,000 USDC
  4. 4

    Execute

    Once a quorum confirms, any director executes. The board can also cancel a transaction by quorum.

Who can act for a seat 4

AI directors 5

AI on the board, inside limits anyone can check.

  • It holds a seat because holders chose it

    An AI director is seated the same way a person is: by delegation. If delegators move their weight, it loses the seat and the next Explorer takes it after 1 block.

  • Its key does only what the scope allows

    The Chamber contract enforces each session key's scope and expiry. Treasury Agent's key can submit, execute, revoke and cancel. It can't confirm.

  • It confirms through a policy account

    To count toward quorum, the agent confirms through a Safe. The Safe signs only when the transaction fits the agent's published mandate and a human co-signer approves. Today people check the mandate; a contract that enforces it comes later.

  • Drafts are text. Approvals are calldata.

    A model can draft a proposal. Every director, human or AI, confirms the decoded call and its simulation, never the model's summary of it.

LORE supply 6

LORE supply sits behind a public, capped queue.

No one can mint LORE on the spot, including the Loreum team. The token is owned by a small mint gate. Loreum DAO can ask for a mint, the request waits 7 days in public, a separate guardian can cancel it, and no quarter can go over 1,000,000 LORE.

Coming later · only if customers ask 7

One board governing spokes on Base and Arbitrum.

If organizations running on Loreum need it, one board on Ethereum will govern Safe accounts on other chains. Every action there starts as a quorum-approved proposal at home. The spoke waits out a delay that a guardian can cancel, then runs only allowlisted calls. Relayers deliver messages; they can't approve anything.

Not available today, and not scheduled. We start only if a go/no-go review finds all of this: at least 3 outside organizations on Loreum, each with 3 or more independent directors and at least one real proposal a week for 8 weeks; at least 3 of them hold assets or admin roles on two or more chains and ask for it; and the audit is closed with no open critical or high findings.

System status and alerts

What the Loreum team can't do

  • Can'tMint LORE. The mint gate owns the token.
  • Can'tMove funds out of any Chamber. Only that Chamber's board quorum can.
  • Can'tUpgrade or pause your Chamber. Each Chamber owns its own upgrade admin.

What the team can do, in public

  • CanChange the implementation that new Chambers are created from. The Team Safe (4-of-7) does this through a 48-hour timelock, so every change is visible first.
  • CanDelegate its own LORE like any holder. The Team Safe holds a large share, so it influences who sits on Loreum DAO's board.
  • CanRun the app, the indexer and the watcher. If they stop, your Chamber keeps working and you can act from any wallet.

Design notes

  1. Positioning comes from the review: accountable boards for onchain treasuries, with humans and AI directors in one queue and delegation deciding who sits. It replaces the current "Decentralized Governance System" headline and the unscoped "compliant" and "credibly neutral" language. This page is the public face of the strategy v2.0 rewrite (D-12). The look evolves landing/src (Bellefair, space navy, star field, square tracked buttons) instead of borrowing the app console. It keeps the rules that apply to every surface: weights 300/400/600 only, depth from layered surfaces rather than drop shadows, and the authority chain as the signature element.
  2. The product window shows real state, not a stock illustration. It's Loreum DAO's board and queue as chamber-indexer reports them (I-12/I-14), with the freshness line, so the landing page costs no RPC either (I-21). The numbers match _mock-data.md. Dogfooding the LORE Chamber is the first customer the review recommends.
  3. The contract rules are stated exactly: quorum 1 + n*51/100, SEATING_DELAY = 1 block, SEAT_UPDATE_TIMELOCK = 7 days, and a 30-day transaction max age. Step 3 describes the shipped #260 decoding and simulation gate.
  4. The authority chain is the signature element on public pages too. The copy says "session key", never "operator", because operator is reserved for the session-key feature and relayers are a separate role (P-8/P-9). Keys can only be set by a contract owner, and an NFT transfer clears them.
  5. AI directors are the differentiator the review keeps. Scope 0x35 omits CONFIRM, and the agent confirms only through its policy account. ERC-8004 is labelled display-only. The page never implies natural-language signing: a model drafts, and directors confirm decoded calldata. The copy separates what a contract enforces (session-key scope and expiry, in Chamber.sol) from what people enforce (the mandate, checked by the policy Safe's human co-signer). An agent account that enforces mandates on-chain is Phase 3 in the review, so the page doesn't claim one. The mandate matches Mandate v2 on app-session-keys.html.
  6. Supply transparency (M-3, M-14, M-15): LORE is owned by LoreMintGate, never directly by a Chamber and never by the team. Delay, window, cap, guardian and dual-control exits all match the gate spec. The budget and op #3 come from the indexer.
  7. Multichain is shown as gated and conditional. It has no dates and no latency promise (the review cut the "15–30 s" claim). It is not framed as bridging or intents, and it is hub-and-spoke over native messengers (D-8, P-19). The gate text restates the review's full Phase 2 gate (P-4/P-7): at least 3 outside orgs with 3+ independent directors and a real proposal a week for 8 weeks, at least 3 of them multichain and asking for it, and the audit closed with 0 open critical/high. Relayers only deliver messages; they never authorize.
  8. Trust links go to one page (eco-trust.html). The only audit wording is the placeholder line. The can/can't lists reflect C-6 (Factory owned by the Team Safe through a 48h timelock), M-2 (4-of-7 Safe, independent guardian) and each Chamber owning its ProxyAdmin. The markers are words ("Can't", "Can"), not a green check next to a power the team doesn't have. The "can" column admits that the Team Safe's LORE influences seating (M-12), because that is the first thing a skeptical reader would ask. The footer has no docs.loreum.org link because that host is broken (D-5). The whole page avoids the banned claims: audited, staking, yield, instant cross-chain and intents (D-7).
